## The Slow Build Nobody Acted On

For six consecutive years, France's digital fitness user base grew at a pace that felt manageable — roughly one million new users per year. That kind of linear growth is easy to acknowledge and easy to defer acting on. Most operators did exactly that. The numbers were real but not urgent. That calculation is about to expire.

The projected user count reaches 9 million by 2027. Then, in a single year, it jumps to 30 million. That is not a forecast rounding error. It reflects a structural shift — the convergence of maturing wearable hardware, mainstream health consciousness, insurance incentives, and platform consolidation that has been quietly building beneath the surface of a steady trend line.

## What the Shape of This Curve Tells You

The shape matters more than any individual number. A market that triples in one year is not simply a bigger version of what existed before — it is a different market. Participant profiles change. Expectations change. The baseline a user brings into your studio changes.

By 2028, a significant portion of your incoming members will already have months or years of biometric history, personalised programming experience, and on-demand content access from digital platforms. They will not arrive as blank slates. They will arrive with standards.

For coaches, this is both a threat and a brief competitive window. The threat is obvious: commoditised content, algorithmic coaching, and low-cost digital substitutes will absorb the casual or budget-constrained user. The window is this — a digitally fluent member is also a member who understands what quality coaching delivers that an app cannot. Your job is to be legible to that member before the market is saturated with operators making the same pitch.

## What Operators Should Do With This

The next three years — while the user base sits between 6 and 9 million — are the preparation phase. This is when digital platforms still want studio partnerships, when member expectations are still formable, and when the cost of building hybrid infrastructure is manageable.

Three priorities stand out. First, integrate, do not compete. Your studio experience should connect with the digital tools your members already use, not pretend they do not exist. Second, make your coaches irreplaceable in ways the data cannot capture — accountability, real-time correction, community, and emotional coaching are not features a wearable ships with. Third, own your member data. When 30 million users are inside digital fitness ecosystems by 2028, the operators who can personalise at depth will retain. The ones relying on platform traffic will compete on price.

The inflection is coming. The operators who read this curve early will shape what the French fitness market looks like on the other side of it.
