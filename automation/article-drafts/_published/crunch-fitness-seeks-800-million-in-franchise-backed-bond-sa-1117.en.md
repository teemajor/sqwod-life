## Crunch Fitness moves to raise $800 million against its franchise royalties

Crunch Fitness is looking to raise approximately $800 million through a bond sale backed by its franchise revenue, [Athletech News](https://athletechnews.com/crunch-fitness-seeks-to-borrow-800-million-in-abs-bond-sale/) reported, citing Bloomberg. The structure includes $650 million in bonds with an average life of around five years targeted at institutional investors, a $100 million privately placed note available to draw at a later date, and a $50 million variable funding facility. Guggenheim Securities is leading the transaction. Early price discussions on the five-year bonds are for spreads of 2.3 to 2.5 percentage points over benchmark rates, according to people with knowledge of the matter who spoke to Bloomberg. None of the terms are final. A representative for Crunch did not respond to a request for comment, and a Guggenheim spokesperson declined to comment.

This type of financing is known as whole-business securitization, a structure that converts predictable royalty and fee streams into tradeable debt. Planet Fitness, one of Crunch's closest competitors by scale and business model, is cited by Bloomberg as a pricing comparison for the proposed deal. That parallel matters: Planet Fitness has used securitized debt repeatedly to fund its own growth, and its existence as a benchmark suggests lenders view Crunch's franchise cash flows as comparably stable. For independent gym and studio operators, the relevance is indirect but real. When large franchise networks access cheap capital at scale, they can accelerate new location openings, depress membership pricing, and increase marketing spend in markets where smaller operators compete directly. The Sqwod Deal Tracker shows $1.6 billion in disclosed gym and studio deals tracked since January 2026, covering names including Equinox, PureGym, and FitLab, which signals that capital is actively moving across the fitness sector at multiple levels, not only at the top.

## What operators should read into a deal this size

The scale of this raise is significant even by fitness industry standards. At $800 million, it dwarfs the typical studio funding round and sits closer to infrastructure or real estate financing in its ambition. The whole-business securitization structure rewards networks that have high franchise unit counts, low franchisee default rates, and long contract durations. Crunch has built exactly that kind of base. For operators who compete with Crunch franchisees locally, this capital could translate into faster unit growth in their markets over the next two to three years. For operators considering their own franchise models, the deal is a demonstration of how mature franchise royalty streams can eventually become a financing asset in their own right.

## Sqwod's take: franchise scale is becoming a capital moat

The story here is not just about one bond sale. It is about the widening gap between operators who have built franchise systems large enough to access institutional debt markets and those who have not. Crunch is effectively monetizing the trust of its franchisees at a price that most competitors cannot match. Planet Fitness has done this before. If Crunch closes this deal on the terms described, it confirms that whole-business securitization is becoming a standard tool for the top tier of the gym franchise world, not an exception. Independent operators and emerging chains do not lose immediately, but every dollar raised this way widens the resource gap. The operators most at risk are mid-size regional chains with no franchise model and no equivalent access to institutional capital. The winners, at least in the short term, are Crunch and the institutional investors who get a yield-bearing asset tied to one of the largest gym networks in the United States.
