## Wearables Move From Gadget to Gym Infrastructure

Fitness tracking has quietly shifted from a personal habit into a core part of how health clubs operate, according to Club Solutions. Members already arrive at the gym with data on their wrists, and brands like Orangetheory Fitness and Crunch Fitness are building that reality into their member experience rather than ignoring it. The model is straightforward: make fitness data visible during the workout, give staff the tools to act on it, and give members a clear picture of their progress over time. For Orangetheory Fitness specifically, wearable technology helps members understand how their bodies respond to exercise in real time, turning a standard class into a personalized feedback loop.

The investment case for wearables in fitness is not hypothetical. The Sqwod Deal Tracker recorded an undisclosed raise by Oura in July 2026, signaling continued capital interest in the wearable health data space. That funding activity sits alongside broader consolidation in wellness, including Informa's acquisition of the Global Wellness Summit and Global Wellness Institute in September 2026 for an undisclosed amount. Capital keeps moving toward the intersection of data, health, and member experience, which is exactly the territory Orangetheory Fitness and Crunch Fitness are operating in.

What remains less clear is how smaller independent studios and boutique operators translate this approach without the infrastructure those larger brands have built. The tools exist, but the integration layer, staff training, and data workflows that make tracking actionable are not yet widely documented for operators running one or two locations.

## Tracking Data as a Coaching and Retention Layer

Club Solutions frames the opportunity around personalization and motivation. When members can see their effort quantified and compare it to previous sessions, they have a concrete reason to return. For operators, that means fitness tracking is not just a feature to advertise but a system that can support the conversations coaches and trainers have with members every day. Real-time data during class and longitudinal progress data between visits serve different purposes, and the clubs getting the most from wearables appear to be using both. The result, according to the reporting, is a stronger relationship between the member and the facility, not just the member and their device.

## Sqwod's Take

The clubs that win here are the ones that treat data as a coaching resource rather than a marketing prop. Orangetheory Fitness has an advantage because its entire class format was designed around heart rate zones, so wearable integration is native to the experience rather than bolted on. Crunch Fitness is a different animal, a large-footprint gym with a broader membership base, which makes the personalization challenge harder but the retention upside potentially larger.

The risk for smaller operators is being caught in the middle. They lack the brand infrastructure of an Orangetheory Fitness but also lack the scale that makes broad data programs financially viable for a Crunch. The smarter move for independent studio owners is to focus narrowly: pick one data point that maps directly to the result their members care about most, build a simple feedback ritual around it, and make that the habit. That is achievable without enterprise software. The studios that wait for a perfect platform will still be waiting while their members leave for somewhere that already shows them their progress.
