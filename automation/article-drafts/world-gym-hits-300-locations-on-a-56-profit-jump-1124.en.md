## World Gym Posts 56% Profit Rise as It Crosses 300 Locations

World Gym Corporation reported a 56% jump in third-quarter net profit, reaching NT$177 million (approximately $5.61 million), according to [Athletech News](https://athletechnews.com/world-gym-passes-300-locations-as-q3-profit-jumps/). Revenue rose 5.5% year over year to NT$2.98 billion (approximately $93.9 million), while gross margin climbed 3.27 percentage points to 19.98% and operating margin improved to 8.95%. The Taiwan-based company, which acquired the global World Gym brand in 2024, now operates more than 300 locations worldwide. Its board approved a quarterly cash dividend of NT$1.455 per share, signalling confidence in continued cash generation. Growth was driven by membership gains, personal training revenue and the addition of Pilates and Hyrox programming across its network.

## Why Margin Improvement Matters More Than the Location Count

Passing 300 locations is a headline number, but the margin story is what operators should study. World Gym grew revenue by 5.5% yet lifted net profit by 56%, meaning it is extracting significantly more value from each additional dollar of revenue than it was a year ago. That gap between revenue growth and profit growth points to tighter cost control, higher-yield revenue streams such as personal training, and programming additions that attract members without proportional cost increases. The Sqwod Deal Tracker has recorded 31 gyms and studios deals with $1.7 billion in disclosed value, including transactions involving YouFit Gyms, Holmes Place Germany and Equinox, reflecting sustained investor appetite for scaled fitness operators. World Gym's results add evidence that disciplined margin management, not just site count, is what makes a fitness chain attractive at scale. For independent operators and regional chains, the relevant question is whether their own revenue mix is shifting toward higher-margin services or remaining flat on membership fees alone.

## Sqwod's Take: Programming Additions Are Doing Real Work Here

The detail most operators should not skim past is the Pilates and Hyrox addition. These are not cosmetic changes to the brand. Hyrox in particular carries a training community with high engagement and willingness to pay for coached programming, while Pilates continues to draw a demographic that gym floors traditionally undersell. World Gym is using both to lift revenue per member rather than simply opening more doors. The risk in this model is execution at scale: a franchise network of 300-plus locations cannot guarantee programming quality uniformly, and a poor Hyrox or Pilates experience damages the brand faster than a standard gym floor would. The operators who win from watching this result are those running mid-size networks with room to introduce one structured programme category and the coaching staff to back it up. The operators who lose are those treating programming as a marketing layer rather than a revenue line.
