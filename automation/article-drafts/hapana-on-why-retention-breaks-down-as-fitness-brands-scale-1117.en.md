## Retention Works Differently at Five Locations Than at One

According to the [Health & Fitness Association](https://www.healthandfitness.org/why-member-retention-gets-harder-as-fitness-businesses-grow/), Jody Hinman, North America Director of Sales at Hapana, joined HFA VP of Marketing & Communications Pam Kufahl to examine how member retention changes as fitness businesses expand. The core argument is straightforward: what works when an owner knows every member by name at a single site does not automatically carry over when that business grows to five, ten, or twenty locations. Personal connection is a retention strategy in itself, but it does not scale. As the business grows, maintaining a consistent member experience, keeping strong staff, and tracking what is actually happening site by site all become harder and more important at the same time.

Hinman pointed to the first 30 days of membership as a critical window. The Health & Fitness Association reported that successful operators are doing something different during that period, using early engagement to reduce the risk of members quietly disappearing before they ever feel embedded in the community. Location-level insights, the conversation suggested, are what allow multi-site operators to spot at-risk members before they cancel rather than after.

Hapana raised $7.25 million in January 2026, according to the Sqwod Deal Tracker, a round that positions it to build out exactly these kinds of tools for operators managing multiple sites. The timing is relevant. The broader market tells a similar story: the Sqwod Deal Tracker has tracked more than 30 fitness and wellness deals since early 2026, with $1.6 billion disclosed in total across brands including Equinox, PureGym, and FitLab. Scale is clearly where investment is flowing, which means the retention problems Hapana is describing are becoming more common, not less.

## The 30-Day Window Multi-Site Operators Cannot Afford to Ignore

The operational implication here is specific. Retention risk at a growing business is not evenly distributed across the membership lifetime. It concentrates early, particularly in the first month, when a new member is deciding whether the habit is going to stick. Operators who lack systems to monitor engagement at the individual location level are effectively blind during that window. A member at a second or third site may be drifting without anyone at the main office noticing. By the time the cancellation comes through, the opportunity to intervene has passed.

What the Health & Fitness Association conversation highlights is that the fix is not simply hiring more staff or sending more emails. It is about having visibility into what is happening at each site and acting on that data with enough speed to matter.

## Sqwod's Take: Technology Vendors Win When Operators Stop Trusting Their Gut

There is an honest tension in this conversation worth naming. Hapana is a software vendor, and the solution it is describing to a retention problem is, not surprisingly, better software. That does not make the analysis wrong. The underlying problem, that personal relationships do not scale and that multi-site operators often lack granular visibility, is real and well documented across the industry. But operators should recognise that the shift being described here is a shift in dependency, from owner intuition to platform data. Businesses that build that capability thoughtfully will likely retain more members. Those that buy a platform without changing their onboarding culture will not. The winners are operators who treat the first 30 days as a system, not a feeling.
