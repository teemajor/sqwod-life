## Ben Davies buys into Muto as the brand targets elite sport

Tottenham Hotspur and Wales defender Ben Davies has acquired a stake in Muto, a British supplements brand focused on midlife health and longevity, according to [City AM](https://www.cityam.com/tottenham-star-acquires-stake-in-british-midlife-supplements-brand-muto/). [Dealroom](https://app.dealroom.co/news/note/spurs-captain-ben-davies-backs-uk-longevity-brand-muto) reported Davies holds the captain's armband at Spurs and is backing the brand as it pushes into elite sporting circles. Financial terms of the deal have not been disclosed.

Muto's move is straightforward: attach a credible, still-active professional athlete to a product aimed at people navigating the physical realities of midlife, then use that association to open doors in elite sport. The brand is not chasing teenagers buying pre-workout. It is positioning itself in the same conversation as recovery, joint health, and performance longevity, all areas that sit squarely inside what fitness studios now sell to their 35-to-55-year-old members.

## Why this matters to studio and gym operators

The deal is a small equity transaction, but it points to something larger. The midlife and longevity segment is attracting both capital and high-profile names at the same time. For context, the Sqwod Deal Tracker has recorded more than 31 gyms and studios deals since January 2026, with $1.7 billion in disclosed value across brands including Equinox and Fitness Park Group. That volume reflects sustained investor appetite for fitness businesses that can serve older, higher-spending demographics. A supplements brand making the same demographic bet, backed by a professional footballer, fits the same thesis from a different angle.

For studio operators, the practical implication is competitive. Muto is not selling memberships, but it is competing for the attention, trust, and wallet share of exactly the members studios most want to retain. If a brand can build a daily habit around a supplement and wrap it in the credibility of elite sport, it earns a place in a member's routine that a studio has to work harder to match. Operators who ignore the longevity supplement category risk ceding part of the relationship they have with their best members to consumer brands that are investing heavily in that space.

## Sqwod's take: supplement brands are becoming retention rivals

The Muto deal is easy to dismiss as a small athlete endorsement story. That would be a mistake. What Davies and Muto are doing is building a credible daily touchpoint with midlife adults who also hold gym memberships. Every time a member takes a Muto product because a professional athlete they respect is behind it, that is a moment of loyalty that did not go to a studio.

The businesses that win here are supplement brands that move fast enough to claim the longevity positioning before it gets crowded, and the studios that are sharp enough to treat supplementation and recovery as part of their own offer rather than someone else's. The businesses that lose are operators who assume a membership is the whole relationship. It has not been for a while, and deals like this one make that clearer.
