## Get A Drip's founder bets £1.5m on preventative care

Richard Chambers, founder of Get A Drip, and NHS GP Dr Luke Pratsides are launching Ohas, a £1.5 million preventative healthcare clinic in west London, according to [Health Club Management](https://www.healthclubmanagement.co.uk/health-club-management-news/Get-A-Drip-founder-launches-15m-preventative-health-clinic-Ohas/363952). The venture pairs a consumer wellness brand with clinical credibility, positioning Ohas as a destination for health optimisation rather than acute treatment. The investment signals that at least some founders who built businesses on accessible, lifestyle-led health services are now moving upstream into more structured, medically supervised models.

For context, the Sqwod Deal Tracker shows £1.6 billion in disclosed gym and studio deals since January 2026 across operators including PureGym, Equinox and Fitness Park Group. Ohas sits at a very different scale, but its £1.5 million price tag is notable precisely because it is not chasing volume. It is a deliberate, focused bet on a clientele willing to pay for prevention over prescription. What remains unknown is the clinic's pricing structure, membership model, or whether Chambers plans to replicate the site beyond west London.

## Why preventative clinics are now competing for the same wallet as premium gyms

Fitness studios and health clubs have long positioned themselves as the front line of preventative health. That argument gets harder to make when a credentialed GP is sitting in a clinic two streets away offering blood panels, metabolic testing and personalised health plans under the same preventative banner. Ohas is not a gym, but it is fishing in the same discretionary spending pool. High-income, health-conscious consumers in urban markets are increasingly looking for joined-up health management, not just a good class timetable. Clinics like Ohas give them a clinical anchor that most studios cannot match.

The opportunity, though, is partnership rather than competition. Operators who can build referral relationships with preventative clinics, or who can demonstrate that their programming supports outcomes those clinics care about, such as cardiovascular health, metabolic markers or musculoskeletal resilience, gain a credibility advantage over competitors who stay purely in the fitness lane.

## The operators who move toward clinical partnerships early will be harder to displace

The launch of Ohas is a signal worth taking seriously. The blending of consumer wellness and clinical medicine is accelerating, and the founders behind it are not academics or hospital groups. They are people who built accessible, scalable health brands and are now adding medical infrastructure on top. That is a competitive posture that traditional fitness operators have rarely adopted and rarely had to face.

Sqwod's view: the winners here are premium operators who act as connective tissue between a member's daily fitness habit and their broader health picture. Studios that can demonstrate measurable health outcomes, facilitate warm introductions to clinical partners, or co-locate services will deepen member loyalty in a way that class variety alone cannot. The losers are mid-market clubs that offer neither the price accessibility of budget operators nor the health credibility of clinic-adjacent premium brands. Ohas is one clinic, but it represents a direction of travel that will not reverse.
