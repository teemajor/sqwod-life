Crunch Fitness targets $800M through franchise-backed bond sale
Crunch Fitness is seeking to raise about $800 million through a whole-business securitisation, a structure in which bonds are backed by predictable franchise revenue streams rather than physical assets. Guggenheim Securities is leading the deal. The planned structure includes $650 million of bonds with an average life of around five years sold to institutional investors, a $100 million privately placed note drawable at a later date, and a $50 million variable funding facility. Early investor talks have begun, though terms are not finalised and may change.
For studio operators and franchise investors, the deal signals that franchise-fee revenue is being treated as a bankable, bond-grade asset. That matters because it reflects how lenders assess the durability of the low-cost, high-volume gym model at scale.

Pete Bates, founder of The Pete Bates Project, published a guest essay on the Global Wellness Institute's platform reflecting on how an inability to articulate thoughts and feelings drove him toward physical confrontation as a young man. He describes the behaviour not as strength but as a failure in learned responses to being challenged, and states it does not excuse any act of violence.

Fitvertise aggregates ad inventory across 2,200-plus gyms in Germany
Fitvertise, a Hamburg-based company, has built an advertising network spanning more than 2,200 fitness studios in Germany, including LifeFit Group, EASYFITNESS, and independent owner-operated studios. Brands can book analogue and digital formats across the entire network through a single contact, while studios join at no cost and receive a share of the resulting ad revenue. For independent studio operators too small to appear in a national media plan on their own, the network offers a way to access brand advertising budgets that have historically bypassed gyms: German government figures show companies spent around 2.7 billion euros on sports sponsorship in 2019.
