When your pricing model serves the deck, not the member
The $19.99 car wash play is a masterclass in recurring-revenue theater: low price = high signup volume = beautiful MRR chart = happy investors. The cars may or may not get clean. Sound familiar? Fitness memberships can fall into exactly the same trap — architect your tiers around churn metrics and cohort retention curves for a future raise, and you quietly stop asking whether the product actually solves the member's problem week to week.
The so-what: audit your membership structure this quarter with one brutal question — 'Would I design this the same way if I never planned to raise money or sell?' If the answer is no, your pricing architecture is sending the wrong signal to your most valuable asset: the people who actually show up. Retention isn't a metric you report; it's a feeling your members have on the way home.

Sqwod recs
✓ Steal: Run a 'member effort score' survey: one question — 'How easy is it to get value from your membership?' — sent 30 days post-signup. Low scores predict churn weeks before it shows in your data.
✓ Track: Start logging your monthly revenue-per-active-member alongside total MRR — it exposes whether growth is real or just volume masking declining engagement per head.
✓ Try: Map one 'uncomfortable physical moment' in your facility — temperature, noise, parking, lighting — and fix it before adding any new programming. Environment retention is invisible until it isn't.
