KINSYN launches a data-driven longevity platform with Hemsworth
Chris Hemsworth co-founded and launched KINSYN, a health platform that pulls together bloodwork, wearable data, health history, and personal goals to build individualised wellness plans. The platform sells supplements, performance support, and physician-guided treatments covering weight management, skin health, and hair health. Its most detailed entry point is a comprehensive biomarker panel that tests more than 50 markers across metabolic, cardiovascular, thyroid, hormone, inflammation, stress, iron, and nutrient categories, with testing available at a lab or at home through a licensed phlebotomist.
For studio and gym operators offering nutrition or recovery services, KINSYN represents a well-funded, celebrity-backed model competing directly for the same client spending. Studios that do not already provide some form of data-driven health programming may find clients drawn toward platforms like this for guidance they once sought from their coach.

Chris Hemsworth launches KINSYN, a personalized longevity platform
Actor and co-founder Chris Hemsworth launched KINSYN, a health platform that combines bloodwork, wearable data, health history, and individual goals to build personalized wellness plans. The platform offers supplements, performance support, and physician-guided treatments for weight management, skin, and hair health, with its most detailed entry point being a comprehensive biomarker panel testing more than 50 markers across metabolic, cardiovascular, thyroid, hormone, inflammation, stress, iron, and nutrient categories, administered at a lab or at home by a licensed phlebotomist. The launch signals growing consumer appetite for data-driven, individualized health programs that go beyond general fitness, which studios and coaches offering nutrition or recovery services may want to monitor as a competing model for client spend.

Nike Strength adds $2,199 Voltra I smart resistance trainer to its lineup
Dimension 6 Fitness, the Nike licensee behind Nike Strength and Jordan Strength equipment, has partnered with fitness technology company Beyond Power to sell Beyond Power's Voltra I portable cable trainer on NikeStrength.com, priced at $2,199. The Voltra I uses a motor to generate up to 230 pounds of electronic resistance, adjustable in one-pound increments, in a device weighing 12.8 pounds. The move comes as Nike has stated plans for performance sports to generate $4 billion in additional annual revenue by fiscal 2031, signalling that connected strength equipment is becoming part of major brand strategies that studio and gym operators will need to account for.
