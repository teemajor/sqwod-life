Connect the dots
When your cancellation clause becomes your PR crisis
Rigid exit policies feel like retention tools — they're actually reputation time bombs. The Fitness First story isn't really about one unhappy member; it's about what happens when a policy written for revenue protection meets a sympathetic human story and a social media screenshot. The contract wins the argument. The operator loses the narrative.
The fix isn't to offer free cancellations to anyone who asks nicely — it's to audit your hardship and medical exit clauses *before* a journalist does it for you. A clearly written, fairly applied exceptions policy costs you almost nothing and buys enormous goodwill. Ask yourself: if this cancellation ended up on a local news segment, would your policy look reasonable? If the honest answer is no, that's your rewrite queue.

Policy watch
Cancellation fees under the microscope
The Fitness First case is a sharp signal that consumer-protection scrutiny around gym exit fees is intensifying — especially when health conditions are involved. Operators should ensure their cancellation terms are clearly disclosed at point of sale and that medical/hardship exemptions are explicit and consistently applied.
