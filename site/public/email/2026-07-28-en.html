Your talent pipeline beats your franchise deck
One LA Pilates studio is quietly doing what most growth-obsessed operators won't: investing in developing instructors instead of opening new doors. It sounds counterintuitive until you realise that franchises export your brand but dilute your culture, while great instructors multiply your culture and carry it into every room they ever teach in. The constraint was never capital — it was people who truly get it.
For studio founders, this reframes the whole expansion question. Before you model out a second location, ask what your instructor development pipeline actually looks like. If your best teachers leave because there's no upward path, you're not scaling — you're churning. The studios that crack retention (member and staff) tend to be the ones that treat instructor growth as a product, not a perk.

Sqwod recs
✓ Track: Start measuring instructor tenure alongside member retention — if your staff churn rate is high, your member churn will follow within 90 days. Run the numbers monthly.
✓ Steal: Create a simple '50+ onboarding script' for your front desk: different language, different goals, different wins than a 30-year-old. Older adults respond to capability framing ('what you'll be able to do') over aesthetics — update your intake form to reflect that.
✓ Try: Run a 30-minute 'teach-back' session monthly where junior instructors coach a segment in front of peers — it accelerates development faster than shadowing alone and costs nothing but calendar time.
