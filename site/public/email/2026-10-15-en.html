1,000 gyms: what the number hides about how they got there
Everyone celebrates the milestone; almost nobody autopsies the model that made it repeatable. fitbox didn't just franchise — they systematised the unglamorous stuff: lead gen handoffs, trainer onboarding, floor layouts that work without a founder in the room. That's the actual product they sold to franchisees, and it's why the number compounds.
So what does that mean if you're running one or three locations? The test is simple: could your second site open without you on-site for month one? If the answer is no, you don't have a business yet — you have a job with extra rent. Audit one system this week that only works because you're physically present, and start writing it down.

Sqwod recs
✓ Track: Monitor your trial-to-membership conversion rate weekly, not monthly — small drop-offs compound fast and weekly visibility lets you catch script or follow-up failures before they cost a full month of revenue.
✓ Steal: Run a 'founder removal' audit: list every revenue-touching task that requires your personal decision, then rank by frequency. The top item is your first SOP to write — franchises are built one removed dependency at a time.
✓ Try: Test a community-cause campaign tied to a member story — a charity push with a named beneficiary drives member pride and referrals in a way a discount never can; even a modest target publicly posted creates accountability and word-of-mouth.
