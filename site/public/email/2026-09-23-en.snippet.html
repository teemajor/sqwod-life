Connect the dots
House rules are theatre until you enforce them publicly
When a Bengaluru member hurled 20-kg equipment and Cult Fitness cancelled their membership, the real product wasn't discipline — it was signal. Every other member watching (and the internet definitely watched) learned exactly where the line is. Most studios post rules on a laminated sheet and pray. The ones that enforce fast and visibly are building something more durable: a culture with actual teeth.
Here's the operator truth: your code of conduct is only as strong as the last time you acted on it. If your staff hesitated, escalated for thirty minutes, or quietly let it slide, you don't have a policy — you have a suggestion. Audit your incident protocol this week. Who has authority to act? What's the script? Because as the KPMG story proves, the internet will timestamp every second of your team's indecision and fundraise around it.
