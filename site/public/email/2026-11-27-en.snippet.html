The rundown
01
The Carry secures strategic funding from Deep Blue Sports + Entertainment
Women's weighted vest brand The Carry received strategic funding from Deep Blue Sports + Entertainment, which also backs LOVB's San Francisco Signal volleyball team, to scale its vest across female performance and longevity markets. The brand, which launched its purpose-built female-form vest last year, plans to release individual athlete editions, co-branded team drops, and women's rucking events. With Amazon searches for weighted vests up 285% and the market projected to reach $2.3 billion by 2036, studio operators and coaches are seeing growing demand from female clients who are adopting vests for rucking, workouts, and everyday use.
Source: Fitt Insider →
02
BowFlex bets on strength training surge and Ryan Reynolds for comeback
BowFlex, the at-home fitness equipment maker, is pursuing a revival by aligning with actor Ryan Reynolds and pointing to rising consumer interest in strength training as its rationale, according to Athletech News. No financial figures or launch dates were reported. For equipment brands and gym operators, the move signals that strength training demand is now prominent enough to anchor a major marketing strategy.
Source: Athletech News →
03
Urban Gym Group opens first UK TrainMore club in Marlow
Urban Gym Group launched its TrainMore brand in the UK with a health club in Marlow, Buckinghamshire, which opened on 19 November 2026. The opening marks TrainMore's entry into the UK market.
Source: Health Club Management →
04
Ben Davies and Shaun Gregory back UK longevity brand MUTO with angel investment
Tottenham Hotspur and Wales captain Ben Davies and senior media executive Shaun Gregory invested in British longevity supplements brand MUTO on 26 November 2026, with Gregory also joining as an advisor. The announcement coincided with MUTO's flagship NAD+ Cellular Complex, a supplement targeting cellular health, receiving Informed Sport certification, meaning it has been independently tested for more than 285 substances banned in sport. For supplement brands and gym operators selling recovery or longevity products, Informed Sport certification is increasingly a baseline requirement for reaching elite sport and professional athlete markets.
Source: Dealroom →
05
Ben Davies takes stake in British supplements brand Muto
Tottenham Hotspur and Wales footballer Ben Davies acquired a stake in Muto, a British supplements brand aimed at midlife consumers, as the company moves to target elite sport. The deal was reported on 26 November 2026. For supplement brands and fitness operators, the move signals growing athlete interest in the midlife wellness category as a route to broader credibility and market reach.
Source: City AM →
06
Five holds Heimat Event 2026 in Donaueschingen on 21 November
Five, a fitness and training concept, brought together studio operators, trainers and industry experts at the Donauhallen in Donaueschingen on 21 November 2026 under the theme 'Let the spark jump.' Wolf Harwath and Wolfgang Packi presented on muscle length training, functional training and biokinematics; Felix Thönnessen spoke on marketing, sales and entrepreneurship; and Katarina Witt made an appeal to the industry to work together for health. Five has stated it wants to put muscle length training more prominently on the agenda and recruit fitness studios and physiotherapy practices as multipliers for that approach.
Source: fitness MANAGEMENT →

Informed Sport certification now a practical threshold for elite markets
MUTO's NAD+ Cellular Complex received Informed Sport certification, confirming independent testing against more than 285 substances banned in sport. For operators and brands supplying supplements to professional or competitive athletes, this type of independent certification is increasingly a minimum requirement rather than a differentiator.
