Big-box money can't buy what you already have
When a franchise drops $100 million on expansion, the instinct is to panic. Don't. Scale is a blunt instrument — it multiplies locations, not relationships. Every new cookie-cutter box that opens near you is also a billboard reminding locals that something more personal exists. The operators who win the next five years won't out-spend the giants; they'll out-know their members in ways no franchise playbook can replicate.
So this week's move isn't defensive — it's offensive. Audit what your big-box competitor literally cannot offer: your coach's name on a check-in text, the program adjustment made because you remembered someone's knee surgery, the community that shows up when someone's having a rough month. These aren't soft perks. They're structural moats. Document them, market them explicitly, and price accordingly — because the franchise wave doesn't kill differentiated studios, it culls the ones that forgot to differentiate.
