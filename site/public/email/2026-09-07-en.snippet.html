Connect the dots
Treating female clients as a sub-category is a revenue leak
Nua just closed a $50M round from Peak XV and Filter Capital — not a lifestyle bet, a structural one. Investors are pricing in a world where women's health is its own vertical, not a pink-label add-on. The founders who spotted this early are now sitting on category-defining businesses.
For studio operators, the signal is uncomfortably clear: if your programming, pricing, and communication still treat female clients as a flavour of 'general fitness,' you're not just leaving money on the table — you're leaving the table entirely. Audit one thing this week: does your onboarding actually ask about cycle-phase training, perimenopause, or postnatal recovery? If not, a competitor who did just read that $50M headline and took notes.
