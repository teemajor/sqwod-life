audioScript: "Orangetheory Fitness has trained its entire global coaching staff of more than 6,500 coaches to support members who are taking GLP-1 weight-loss medications, in partnership with the National Academy of Sports Medicine, known as NASM. The brand says it is the first fitness company to do this at that scale. GLP-1 medications are injectable or oral drugs that reduce appetite and promote weight loss. NASM has more than 35 years of experience training fitness professionals. Around one in eight American adults is currently on a GLP-1 medication, and more than half say the medication alone is not enough for lasting results. Studio operators who build this kind of coach expertise may find it easier to retain and attract that growing member group.\n\nNext, Australian reformer Pilates franchise at-Pilates 24/7 plans to open its first two United States studios in Miami and Dallas in early 2027. The brand was founded in 2021 by Tara and Russell Jarrett and currently operates 60 studios across Australia and New Zealand, serving around 12,000 members. Its model gives members access to reformer machines at any hour, using individual screens and headphones and a library of more than 700 on-demand classes, rather than scheduled instructor-led sessions. The company has sold more than 80 franchise territories in the Asia-Pacific region and plans to offer franchise opportunities in Florida and Texas after the first two locations open. Operators in those markets now have a direct read on how an unstaffed, on-demand reformer format may compete locally.\n\nIn Germany, the boutique Pilates chain YTTP, founded in Cologne, opened its fifth reformer Pilates studio in Berlin on 14 and 15 November. The new location is at Backfabrik, Saarbrücker Strasse 36 to 38, in the Prenzlauer Berg district. It covers 300 square metres and holds 35 reformer machines from the brand Your Reformer, distributed in Germany by Matrix. This opening is one of four studios YTTP plans to launch in the fourth quarter of 2026.\n\nConnected fitness company iFIT has launched a product called Tailor, a voice-based artificial intelligence coach that holds adaptive conversations with members. The company says Tailor is a central part of its strategy going forward.\n\nAnd finally, Canadian kids' snack brand Wellness Monster closed its first seed funding round on November 17, led by investment firm Grip Ventures with participation from angel investors. The amount was not disclosed. The brand was founded by Brittany Compton, a former executive at Mars and former president of Love Child Organics. Wellness Monster launched in June 2025 and now sells allergen-friendly, high-protein snacks, including a product called Power Bites with eight grams of protein, across around 180 retail stores in Canada.\n\nThat's Sqwod Daily for Wednesday.
