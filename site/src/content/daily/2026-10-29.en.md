audioScript: "Intake Breathing has raised sixty-two million dollars to expand its magnetic nasal dilator into mainstream retail, marking one of the larger consumer brand investments in the sleep and recovery space this year.\n\nIntake was founded by medical device engineer Jim Castillo and is led by his son-in-law and chief executive Alex Hauck. The company makes a reusable magnetic band paired with an adhesive tab that mechanically opens the nasal valve to improve airflow. That is different from traditional adhesive nasal strips, which simply pull the nose open from the outside. The money comes from Camino Partners, the investment firm founded by Daniel Lubetzky, who built the Kind Snacks brand. For studio and gym owners building recovery or sleep programming, this raise is a signal that consumer capital is moving seriously into the breathing and recovery category.\n\nNext, Garmin reported fitness segment revenue of six hundred and ninety-four million dollars for its third quarter, ended September 26, 2026. That is up sixteen percent year over year. The segment produced operating income of two hundred and thirty-one million dollars at a thirty-three percent operating margin. Fitness was Garmin's largest business segment by revenue. In July, Garmin launched the Cirqa Smart Band, a screenless wearable priced at one hundred and ninety-nine dollars and ninety-nine cents with no subscription fee. That positions it directly against Whoop, which requires a paid membership. For operators evaluating wearable partnerships or member-facing technology, the subscription-free model is a meaningful contrast to Whoop's ongoing cost structure.\n\nIn Indiana, Gemi Ozdemir opened Ozwell Fitness in Carmel, combining personal training, red light therapy, sauna and other recovery services in one facility using Neo Science wellness technology. Ozdemir said his motivation was frustration with having to visit multiple locations for different services. The club attracted thousands of new members and tripled usage rates for some of its recovery services, with Ozdemir reporting strong member retention. For operators, the results suggest that bundling recovery and training under one roof can lift both engagement and loyalty.\n\nAnd finally, Total Fitness chief executive Sophie Lawler and Fitness First chief executive Marc Diaper spoke at the HCM Summit, a health club management industry event. Both said that delivering value, investing in innovation and protecting human connection are central to operator success.\n\nThat's Sqwod Daily for Thursday. Thanks for listening."
