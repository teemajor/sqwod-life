audioScript: "The American College of Sports Medicine has named wearable technology the top global fitness trend for 2027, for the twenty-first year running its annual survey.\n\nThe ACSM published its 2027 fitness trend survey on 21 October 2026. More than 1,400 fitness professionals rated 50 potential trends. Wearable technology came first. Mobile fitness apps came second, and balance, flow and core training ranked third. The ACSM publishes a full top-ten list, which coaches and studio owners can use to judge which services and tools are drawing the most attention internationally.\n\nNext, a content deal for gym and studio operators. On 14 October 2026, Fitness On Demand, a commercial platform that supplies workout content to studios, gyms and wellness operators, announced a partnership with Mvmnt, a digital fitness platform, in Minneapolis. The deal adds 99 Mvmnt workouts to Fitness On Demand's existing library of more than 1,800 workouts. The new content covers strength, bodyweight training, high-intensity interval training, boxing, dance, cardio, yoga, Pilates, mobility and meditation, with sessions built for different life stages and fitness levels. It will be available on in-facility screens and mobile touchpoints. Studio and gym owners can broaden their programming without hiring additional instructors or adding a separate app.\n\nIn Ireland, a small studio raised money for a good cause. ØRA Wellness Studio in Athlone, run by Aishling Croughan, held a Pink Wellness Morning in support of Breast Cancer Awareness. Croughan organised the event alongside life coach Libby Hayes. It brought women together for gentle movement, breathwork, coffee and cake, with support from local businesses including Mary's SuperValu Athlone, A Room in Bloom and Sláinte Coffee Shop. The morning raised over one thousand euros. The event is a clear example of how studios can work with local businesses and community figures to run charity events that build connection and goodwill.\n\nAnd finally, a funding round in women's health technology. Unravel Health, a women's hormone monitoring company, has raised two and a half million euros to launch a product called the Micro Lab. It is an at-home device that reads estradiol and other biomarkers from a saliva sample in minutes. The device is due to ship in 2027. The company plans to add more hormones and expand into men's health. Coaches who work with clients on hormonal health, including perimenopause or cycle-based training, may eventually have access to daily, detailed data rather than relying on single blood test results.\n\nThat's Sqwod Daily for Wednesday. Thanks for listening."
