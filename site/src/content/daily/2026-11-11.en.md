audioScript: "Harvey Spevak, the chairman of Equinox Group, has organised a six-and-a-half-million-dollar financing round for Vesalius Longevity Labs, a publicly traded longevity company formerly known as Sweet Earth Holdings Corporation. The company completed a private placement of roughly six-point-two million units at one dollar and five cents each. Vesalius plans to use the money to expand its sales team, enter new markets, and grow its platform connecting licensed healthcare providers with peptide therapies and other longevity services. Peptides are short chains of amino acids increasingly used in longevity and performance medicine. Spevak said he believes longevity represents one of the most exciting opportunities in health and wellness over the coming decade. That a major luxury fitness operator is now placing capital into a provider-facing longevity platform is a signal worth watching for gym and studio owners deciding whether to compete or partner in this space.\n\nNext, Nord Gyms will open its first location in Crouch End, North London, in December. The site combines strength and conditioning facilities with personal coaching and contrast therapy, which means alternating between hot and cold recovery environments. Pairing performance training with recovery under one roof is a format more operators in the premium gym segment are testing.\n\nIn access technology, Gantner, a company that makes access control and locker systems for fitness clubs, has launched a facial recognition check-in product called GT7 powered by XS4 Face. A member looks into a camera, the system checks their membership status in the club management software in real time, and the door opens without a card, phone or wristband. The system also supports RFID, QR codes and digital membership cards. Gantner says the product complies with the DSGVO, the European Union data protection regulation. For operators running unstaffed or twenty-four-hour facilities, this kind of automated entry can reduce reliance on front-desk staff and help manage busy periods at the door.\n\nAnd finally, Elevate, a London-based smoothie bar, raised two-point-seven million dollars from JamJar Investments, Soho House founder Nick Jones, and other investors. The brand already sells more than one smoothie per minute and posts over one million dollars in annual sales. It opened a second site inside Selfridges department store in April and has run collaborations with Nike, SKIMS and Adanola. The new funding will support two additional shops opening early next year. As functional nutrition moves closer to what gyms and studios already offer, the way Elevate is building brand reach beyond food retail is worth noting.\n\nThat's Sqwod Daily for Wednesday. Thanks for listening."
