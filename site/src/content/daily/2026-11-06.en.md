audioScript: "The DSSV, Germany's association of fitness and health club operators, released a study on 5 November showing that adults aged 60 to 75 vary far more than operators might assume, in their health, social connection, and motivation to stay active.\n\nThe study, presented at the Federal Press Conference in Berlin, looked at what the researchers call Generation Ü, the 60-to-75 age group. It found real potential in this cohort for continued work and social participation, but wide differences between individuals. For studio owners and coaches, the core warning is practical: treating older adults as one uniform segment is a mistake, and programming for this group needs to reflect genuine differences in capacity and motivation.\n\nNext, HOTWORX founder and CEO Stephen Smith spoke on the Club Solutions podcast with editor Taylor Gabhart about how his infrared fitness brand reached 913 locations, with one thousand in sight. Smith said the recurring monthly dues model, which smooths cash flow by collecting a fixed fee each month, has anchored fitness business models for more than 30 years. He also pointed to a philosophy he calls more workout, less time, reducing session length to save members both time and money. For operators deciding how much technology and programming to layer on, Smith's argument is direct: limiting complexity is itself a growth strategy.\n\nIn the UK, Strong Pilates opened seven studios in 2026, bringing its total to 15 locations, up from eight at the end of 2025. The Australian-born boutique franchise is now targeting 20 new UK studios in 2027. Confirmed sites include Nine Elms in early 2027, with franchisees exploring West Hampstead, St Albans, White City and Richmond. The pace points to strong franchisee demand, particularly across London and the South East.\n\nIn India, the Punjab state government announced free online coaching for the Union Public Service Commission Civil Services Examination and the Punjab Civil Services Examination, targeting one thousand college students across the state. Chief Minister Bhagwant Mann announced the programme, with 750 of the one thousand places reserved for students from financially disadvantaged backgrounds. This story falls outside the fitness industry but was included in today's available sources.\n\nAnd finally, fitness MANAGEMENT today released a new edition of its specialist publication medical fitness and healthcare, known as mfhc. The twice-yearly magazine covers sports medicine, diabetology, rehabilitation, prevention and nutrition, and is distributed to fitness and health facilities across Germany, Austria and Switzerland, as well as to industry companies and trade events.\n\nThat's Sqwod Daily for Friday. Thanks for listening."
