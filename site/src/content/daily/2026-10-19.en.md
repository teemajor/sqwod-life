audioScript: "An Amsterdam startup called Huppa has raised one million euros to build one piece of software that runs a whole gym or studio.\n\nHuppa was started in 2024 by two gym owners, Sharief Mohamed and Gino Arendsz, who were frustrated with the software at their own kickboxing gym and CrossFit box. Their platform puts member management, class scheduling, payments, financial reporting and automation in one system. It connects to the payment provider Mollie and to aggregators such as Urban Sports Club and ClassPass. Studios pay by the number of active customers, with no separate modules. The money is a pre-seed round, which means a company's first outside funding, led by the founders of several Dutch tech companies, with the investor NL SportTech Ventures taking part. If you run a studio on four or five different tools, this is the problem they are trying to solve.\n\nNext, a Canadian Lagree brand is coming to the United States. Reformd, owned by the Toronto operator Sweat and Tonic, plans to open what it says will be the world's largest Lagree studio in Calabasas, California, in January 2027. It will have 37 Lagree machines and six recovery rooms offering massage, cryotherapy, dry floating and compression therapy, and the recovery services will be open to people who do not take classes. The company says its Toronto flagship has recorded more than 126,000 bookings since it opened in June 2025. For studio owners, the detail to note is recovery sold as its own product, not just an add-on to classes.\n\nAnd finally, in Germany: the DSSV, the country's association of fitness and health club operators, is holding an online briefing on 19 November, from 12:30 to 13:30. It covers two prevention courses, Kraft Aktiv, which means active strength, and Cardio-Fitness. Both are free for DSSV members, come with full course manuals, and are certified so that studios can bill them to German health insurers. If you run a studio in Germany, that is a way to get courses paid for by insurers.\n\nThat's Sqwod Daily for Monday. Thanks for listening."
