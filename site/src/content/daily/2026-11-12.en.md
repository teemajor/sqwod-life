Whether these operator-led formats can hold their own alongside Hyrox is still an open question.\n\nNext, gym technology company Groe Solutions has appointed Julio Marshall as its chief commercial officer. Marshall will lead commercial strategy, enterprise sales, revenue operations and strategic partnerships. Groe builds a gym intelligence platform that uses computer vision and management system integrations to give multi-location health club operators a single view of member activity, facility demand and operational data. The hire signals that Groe is pushing to get that platform in front of senior decision makers across the industry.\n\nIn reinsurance, John Schoonbee, global chief medical officer of Swiss Re, the Swiss Reinsurance Group, called on insurers to take a more active role in preventing ill health. He cited worsening metabolic health and slowing improvements in mortality rates as signs that the current model is unsustainable. For fitness operators, this is a signal that large reinsurance capital is beginning to treat prevention as a core business concern, not a secondary one.\n\nAndrew Huberman, the neuroscientist and podcaster, is joining Hims and Hers as a non-independent board member, focused on science and marketing. According to a filing with the Securities and Exchange Commission, the deal is worth up to twenty-four million dollars. That includes seven million dollars for two years of advisory work, fifteen million dollars paid to his media company Scicomm Media for three years of podcast advertising, up to one million dollars a year for optional short-form videos, and fifty thousand dollars in cash plus two hundred and fifty thousand dollars in equity per year as board compensation. Hims and Hers is preparing to roll out products including sermorelin, glutathione, B12, NAD plus and L-carnitine, with BPC-157 and TB-500 to follow. These are peptides, which are short chains of amino acids used to support various health and performance functions. The move shows that consumer health brands are positioning peptides as mainstream products rather than niche biohacking tools, which is territory fitness operators and coaches should be watching.\n\nAnd finally, JETSET Pilates has adopted AXLE, a technology platform built for fitness, wellness and beauty businesses, to coordinate marketing across its network of more than one hundred and fifty franchise locations. The arrangement gives individual franchisees flexibility while keeping brand materials and campaigns consistent. For multi-site franchise operators, it points to the growing role of purpose-built technology in keeping brands aligned as networks scale.\n\nThat's Sqwod Daily for Thursday. Thanks for listening."
