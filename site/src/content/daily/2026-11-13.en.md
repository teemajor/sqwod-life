audioScript: "Men now make up roughly one third of Pilates participants, a marked shift in a category that was long dominated by women. Studios including Club Pilates, STRONG Pilates, and solidcore are adjusting their programming and messaging to serve a co-ed membership. For studio operators, this is a structural change in who is coming through the door, not a short-term trend.\n\nNext, WHOOP launched WHOOP Humans, an in-app social network for its three million members. Members can add friends, share Sleep, Recovery, and Strain scores, upload activities with photos, and comment on posts. Fifty-seven percent of WHOOP members already belong to at least one Team, and members open the app eight times a day. The move puts WHOOP in direct competition with Strava, which has more than two hundred million users and has become the default social layer for fitness activity. Keeping social behaviour inside a wearable platform, rather than sending it to a third-party app, is becoming a clear competitive priority for wearable makers.\n\nOn November 12, the Health and Fitness Association named Greta Wagner its permanent president and chief executive officer at the Beyond Activ Americas event in Dallas. Wagner had served six months in an interim role and led the development of a new three-year strategic plan, built with the HFA Board of Directors and industry stakeholders. The plan is focused on delivering greater member value, strengthening advocacy, and expanding the industry's influence. For gym and studio operators who rely on the HFA for lobbying support and resources, the appointment signals a more focused push to make the association a stronger advocate for the industry.\n\nOn November 9, Ali Kaltman, formerly head of partnerships at Grüns, and Daniel Rabinovich, formerly vice president of brand partnerships at Barry's, launched a platform called PIRL. PIRL matches brands with studios based on fitness discipline, member demographics, and geographic market, and handles contracting and payments. Studio partners already include Pvolve, SoulCycle, and Strong Pilates. Studios list for free. PIRL charges a fee on the brand side and holds funds until class attendance is verified before releasing payment. That structure gives studio operators a no-cost route to brand revenue with a built-in attendance check.\n\nAnd finally, Gantner, an access control technology company, released the GT7 powered by XS4 Face, a facial recognition system built for fitness studios and health practices. Members gain entry with a brief look at a camera, no card, phone, or wristband required. For operators, the system can reduce front-desk workload, ease congestion at peak times, and support unstaffed or low-staff overnight access models.\n\nThat's Sqwod Daily for Friday. Thanks for listening."
