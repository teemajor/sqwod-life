audioScript: "Equinox has named Brendan Whitworth its new chief executive officer, bringing in an outside leader as the premium gym group plans its next phase of expansion.\n\nWhitworth joins from Anheuser-Busch, the brewing company, where he served as chief executive. He will also sit on the Equinox board. Harvey Spevak, who led Equinox from 1999, moves to the role of chairman. Spevak said he was personally involved in recruiting Whitworth and has known him for several years. Whitworth said member loyalty will sit at the centre of his approach, adding: \"People embrace Equinox in their daily lives and routines because of how it makes them feel and what it helps them achieve, and I take seriously the responsibility of upholding that standard of excellence and earning our members' loyalty day in and day out.\" Equinox currently operates one hundred and eighteen clubs across eighteen major markets and reported double-digit revenue growth, with membership numbers and check-ins both at all-time highs. For operators in the premium segment, the leadership change and the scale of planned investment confirm that well-capitalised groups are continuing to expand rather than consolidate.\n\nNext, Mark Mastrov, the founder of 24 Hour Fitness, is building a new club in Las Vegas. Mastrov launched 24 Hour Fitness in 1983 and has spent four decades developing gyms across more than sixty countries. His new project, called Founders Club by 24 Hour Fitness, will open in early 2027 at nine thousand two hundred West Sahara Avenue in the Summerlin neighbourhood of Las Vegas. The facility covers more than forty-five thousand square feet and represents a ten million dollar investment. More than one million dollars of that goes into recovery equipment, which covers things like massage, compression, contrast therapy and similar post-workout tools. Twenty-five thousand square feet is dedicated to free weights and strength training across twelve platforms. Mastrov said Summerlin is one of the fastest-growing communities in the country, and described the project as a demonstration of what the next generation of fitness looks like. For full-service club operators, the fact that more than one million dollars is ring-fenced for recovery infrastructure is a clear signal that recovery is now being treated as a core part of the member offer, not an optional extra.\n\nThat's Sqwod Daily for Tuesday. Thanks for listening."
