## The problem hiding in plain sight

Your clients walk into the gym, glance at the mirror, check their phone, adjust their AirPods, and then wonder why their form is still a mess three months in. The environment is doing the coaching — badly. Two stories this week, from opposite ends of the world, point at the same root cause: most people have never actually *felt* a movement. They've only watched one.

## What Dubai and Arkansas are quietly telling you

A gym in Dubai just launched a workout class in complete darkness — no mirrors, no lights, nothing (Khaleej Times). That's not a gimmick. That's a forced reset. When you strip away visual feedback, the nervous system has to pick up the slack. Proprioception — your body's internal sense of position and tension — suddenly has to do its job instead of outsourcing it to a reflection.

Meanwhile, in Arkansas, a coach is making the case for the Towel Abduction exercise as a tool for reducing injury risk (Arkansas Democrat-Gazette). The movement uses a towel underfoot to create resistance and demand control through the hip abductors. It's low-tech, low-load, and exactly the kind of drill that teaches the body to *feel* where it is in space — not just move through a range of motion.

These two stories aren't just curiosities. They're pointing at the same gap: most training environments reward looking over feeling. Mirrors, screens, and open-floor chaos all pull attention outward. The result? Clients who can perform an exercise but can't own it under fatigue, in the dark, or in the unpredictable conditions where real life and real sport happen.

## The do-this-now fix for your next session

You don't need to black out your gym. You need to steal the *principle*.

**1. Add one eyes-closed set per session.** Pick a stable, low-risk movement — a glute bridge, a split squat, a standing hip hinge. Ask your client to close their eyes for one set. Watch what happens. Their tempo slows. Their breathing changes. They start *listening* to the movement instead of watching it. Debrief after: where did they feel it? Where did they lose it?

**2. Build the Towel Abduction into your warm-up toolkit.** As the Arkansas Democrat-Gazette reports, this exercise targets the hip abductors in a way that demands stability, not just strength. A towel under the foot creates an unstable, low-friction surface that keeps the nervous system honest. It's a five-minute addition that earns its place before any lower-body session.

**3. Frame it for the client.** "We're training your body to feel the movement, not just do the movement." That single sentence changes how clients engage. They stop performing for the mirror and start listening to the feedback loop you're building.

The best coaching environments aren't always the flashiest. Sometimes they're darker, quieter, and more honest than your clients expected. Give them the conditions to actually *feel* their body work — and the results will speak louder than any mirror.
