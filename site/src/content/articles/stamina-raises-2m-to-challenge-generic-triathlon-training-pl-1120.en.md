## Stamina closes $2M seed round with an Olympic champion on the cap table

London-based triathlon training app Stamina has raised $2 million in seed funding, according to [Athletech News](https://athletechnews.com/triathlon-training-app-stamina-raises-2-million-backed-by-olympic-champion/). The round was backed by Olympic cycling champion Greg Van Avermaet, Belgian funds Syndicate One and 100IN, content creator Average Rob, and former professional basketball player Tomas Van Den Spiegel. Founded by Willem Duyck and Shuowang He, Stamina builds personalized training plans that combine swimming, cycling and running, adjusting workouts based on fitness level, available time and race goals. Athletech News reported the app has surpassed 50,000 downloads since launching in 2025.

The funding sits at the smaller end of recent fitness and wellness raises tracked by the Sqwod Deal Tracker, which has logged $1.6 billion in disclosed deals across gyms and studios since January 2026. For context, Persona raised $10M in October 2026 and The Stack pulled in $2.27M the same month. Stamina's $2M round is therefore modest, but its positioning is deliberate: the company is targeting the gap between expensive personal coaching and generic plans that cannot adapt to a changing schedule, a problem Duyck encountered personally when preparing for his first half-distance triathlon in 2023.

## Stamina's algorithm argument reframes what "AI-powered" actually means for endurance coaching

The company draws a clear line between its proprietary training algorithm, developed alongside coaches and professional triathletes Ari Klau and Greg Harper, and generative AI. "Ask an AI chatbot for a training plan twice and you'll often get two different answers," He told Athletech News. "That's fine for writing an email, but not for your training." That distinction matters beyond triathlon. Many fitness operators have moved quickly to market AI-powered features without specifying whether those features are deterministic tools built on structured logic or large language models that produce variable output. Stamina is making consistency and reproducibility a competitive advantage, and naming generative AI as the thing it is not.

## Structured algorithms beat novelty when physical outcomes are on the line

Stamina's raise is small, but the argument it is making is one that every fitness operator buying or building tech should take seriously. The app is not anti-technology. It is anti-inconsistency. The fitness industry has spent the past two years attaching the word AI to nearly everything, and the distinction between a language model that generates novel text and a rule-based system optimised for progressive athletic adaptation has largely been lost in that rush. Stamina is betting that endurance athletes, a group that tracks splits, heart rate zones and recovery windows with unusual precision, will notice the difference and pay for reliability. The operators who win here are those who can make the same argument about their own digital tools: not that they use AI, but that the tool produces a dependable, improving outcome every single session. The operators who lose are those who have added AI-branded features without being able to explain what the system actually does when a member's schedule changes on a Wednesday night.
