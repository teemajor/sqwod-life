## The Middle Is Getting Squeezed Out

There's a brutal sorting happening in fitness right now. On one end, chains are swallowing markets whole. On the other, small gyms are surviving on community loyalty. The operators stuck in the middle — too big to feel local, too small to compete on price — are the ones in trouble.

This week made that split impossible to ignore.

PureGym just acquired NonStop and became the largest low-cost gym operator in Switzerland, according to Well Nation. Meanwhile, fitbox hit 1,000 locations across its brand group, a milestone body LIFE traces from a single site. These aren't flukes. They're the compounding result of a clear strategic bet made years ago: scale fast, standardise everything, win on value and volume.

At the exact same time, a small gym in Flint raised over £3,700 for prostate cancer research after one of its own members was diagnosed, per Leader Live. That's not a marketing campaign. That's a community that would feel the gym's absence like a missing neighbour.

## Two Playbooks, Zero Middle Ground

The franchise model is proving it can industrialise growth without losing the plot. fitbox's run from one to 1,000 sites is a masterclass in systemised expansion — the kind of growth that attracts the employer-level conversations DSSV is now having at the Deutschen Arbeitgebertag 2026. When gym brands reach institutional scale, they earn a seat at policy tables. That's a moat most operators never think about building.

The acquisition playbook is equally aggressive. PureGym in Switzerland and the Martin & Co Huddersfield franchisee completing its fourth agency acquisition (per L'Express Franchise) are both running the same play: buy market presence faster than you can organically build it. It's expensive, it's risky, and right now it's working.

Then there's the community-first gym. No acquisition war chest, no franchise infrastructure. Just a member gets sick, the gym shows up, and £3,700 flows in from people who care. That's retention no loyalty app can replicate.

And fuelling both ends of the market? Supplements. India's The Stack just raised $2.27M to expand its supplements line, per Dealroom. Consumers aren't slowing down on performance nutrition — operators who ignore that revenue stream are leaving money on the table regardless of their size.

## What You Should Actually Do This Week

Stop trying to be everything. Here's how to act depending on where you sit:

**If you're a growing operator or investor:** Study the fitbox and PureGym moves closely. Both scaled through repetition and acquisition, not reinvention. Pick your model — franchise or acquire — and move before the market in your region consolidates around someone else.

**If you're an independent gym:** Your community is your moat. The Flint story isn't heartwarming fluff — it's a retention and differentiation strategy. Engineer those moments. Know your members' lives. Show up when it counts.

**For everyone:** Add a revenue line tied to nutrition. The Stack raising $2.27M signals continued investor and consumer appetite for supplements. Whether you white-label a product or simply stock trusted brands, your members are already spending that money somewhere.

The fitness market is polarising fast. The operators who win will know exactly which game they're playing — and play it without apology.
