## The market just told you something

Two stories dropped this week that look unrelated. One is about a premium athleisure brand chasing a three-way stretch into new categories, per Fast Company. The other is about Vasa Fitness putting inclusive strength training at the center of its entire brand and physical build-out, per Athletech News. Read them together and a single message gets loud: **vague positioning is expensive, and specificity is the new scale.**

Vuori hit a $5.5 billion valuation by owning a feeling — West Coast, performance-meets-leisure, not-your-dad's-gym-clothes. Now Fast Company reports they're stretching into new directions to justify that number. That stretch is a bet. It works if the core identity is strong enough to carry new weight. It fails if no one was ever sure what Vuori stood for in the first place.

Vasa, meanwhile, isn't stretching outward. They're drilling down. Inclusive strength training isn't a campaign — Athletech News makes clear it's shaping the actual club layout, the equipment choices, the visual identity. They're not adding a feature. They're becoming something specific.

## The trap most operators fall into

Here's where coaches and gym owners quietly lose: they try to be the Vuori *before* they've built the Vasa foundation. They chase the expansion — new class formats, new demographics, new revenue streams — without first answering the question every potential member is actually asking: *who is this place actually for?*

A $5.5 billion brand can survive a fuzzy stretch because it has the runway to figure it out. Your facility doesn't. When a prospective member lands on your Instagram, visits your website, or walks through your door for the first time, you have about thirty seconds to make them feel seen. Generic doesn't do that. "We offer something for everyone" is the fitness equivalent of a shrug.

Vasa's move is the smarter template for operators who don't have billion-dollar buffers. Pick your person. Build for them visibly and specifically. Then, once that identity is solid, *then* you stretch.

## Do this before your next planning meeting

Pull up your gym's homepage, your social bio, and the script your front-desk staff uses when someone calls. Ask yourself one question about each: **if a complete stranger read only this, would they know exactly who this place is for?**

If the answer is no — or "kind of" — you have a positioning job to do before you have a marketing job to do.

Here's the exercise:

1. **Name your one person.** Not a demographic — a human. The 42-year-old who never feels welcome in a traditional weight room. The busy parent who needs efficiency over aesthetics. Be specific enough that it feels slightly uncomfortable.
2. **Audit your space through their eyes.** Vasa is doing this physically — what does your floor plan, your signage, your programming *say* to that person?
3. **Write one sentence that only your gym could say.** Not "world-class coaching" or "results-driven training." Something true, specific, and yours.

Vuori will figure out its stretch. They have the valuation to buy time. You've got something better — the agility to get specific fast, before a better-positioned competitor moves into your zip code and makes the choice for you.
