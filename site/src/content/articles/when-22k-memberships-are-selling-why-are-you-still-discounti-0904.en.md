## The market is pulling apart, and the middle needs a reason

In Germany, studio prices already split by type. The average gross membership in 2025 was **€48.55 a month**. Chains averaged **€40.54**, single studios **€59.24** and special-interest studios **€80.02**.<sup class="fn"><a href="#source-1">1</a></sup> Across Europe, the market grew to 75.5 million members and revenue rose 9.1% in 2025.<sup class="fn"><a href="#source-2">2</a></sup>

In the US, the low-price end has grown fastest: Planet Fitness alone had 20.8 million members at the end of 2025.<sup class="fn"><a href="#source-4">4</a></sup>

That does not mean the middle is dead. A UK market analyst estimated that around 70% of UK sites are mid-market and called the "squeeze" over-exaggerated.<sup class="fn"><a href="#source-3">3</a></sup> What it does mean: a mid-priced studio needs a clear reason to cost more than the budget gym down the road. A premium tier is one of the clearest ways to give it one.

## Even budget gyms make their money on the top tier

The best public evidence on tiers comes from the budget chains themselves. At Planet Fitness, **66.5% of recurring-billing members pay for Black Card**, the top tier, and average dues rose from $17.63 in 2021 to $19.51 in 2025.<sup class="fn"><a href="#source-4">4</a></sup> In November 2025 the company announced it would raise Black Card to $29.99 after the 2026 peak joining season.<sup class="fn"><a href="#source-5">5</a></sup>

At Basic-Fit, about **half of new joiners chose a higher tier** after the late-2024 price restructure, and average revenue per membership at its own clubs rose 2.8% to €24.91 a month.<sup class="fn"><a href="#source-6">6</a></sup>

If chains built on low prices grow through their top tier, an independent studio with real coaching has more to put in one.

## What members actually pay more for

Look at what premium operators sell, and a pattern shows up:

- **Life Time** earned **$3,531 per center membership** in 2025, which includes spending in the club, not just dues. Its Signature tier adds small-group training and court time, and its personal training sessions grew 18% to over 220,000 a month.<sup class="fn"><a href="#source-7">7</a></sup>
- **Third Space** in London charges from **£257 to £345 a month**, with more than 270 classes a week on average across its clubs, sauna and steam, and recovery tools such as compression boots; there is also an on-site private sports-medicine practice. Personal training is extra, from £85 an hour, and a recovery membership is a separate add-on.<sup class="fn"><a href="#source-8">8</a></sup>
- **Equinox Optimize**, launched in 2024, costs **$3,000 a month** on top of a regular membership, with a six-month minimum, and bundles three personal training sessions a week, nutrition and sleep coaching, a monthly massage and biomarker testing.<sup class="fn"><a href="#source-9">9</a></sup>

The common thread is not more square metres. It is **more attention from people, recovery, and progress members can see**. Community matters too: 67% of members agreed community is the biggest driver of their motivation and accountability (an ABC Fitness survey summarised by Les Mills).<sup class="fn"><a href="#source-10">10</a></sup>

## How to build your premium tier

**Offer three options, and design the middle one for most people.** When buyers see three options, the middle one tends to gain share. A 2016 meta-analysis found this effect robust, but its size varied a lot, and it tended to be weaker when options traded price against quality, which is exactly what membership tiers do.<sup class="fn"><a href="#source-11">11</a></sup> Don't rely on a fake "decoy" option to push people up: a 2014 study found the decoy effect usually did not appear once buyers saw or experienced the real options.<sup class="fn"><a href="#source-12">12</a></sup> Every tier should be one you would happily sell.

**Fill the top tier with attention, not space.** Practical examples for a studio: a monthly check-in with a coach, a reserved small-group slot, access to recovery equipment, priority booking at busy times. These cost little per member and members notice them every week.

**Make sure the extras get used.** In a classic US study (late-1990s data), members paying over $70 a month averaged 4.3 visits a month, so more than $17 per expected visit, when a 10-visit pass at $10 a visit was available.<sup class="fn"><a href="#source-16">16</a></sup> People buy the intention to train. A premium tier earns its price when it turns that intention into booked sessions, so put the coach check-in and the small-group slot in the calendar, not just on the price list.

**Price it against your own base and your market.** In Germany, the gap between a typical single studio (€59.24) and a special-interest studio (€80.02) shows the room a clearly different offer can command.<sup class="fn"><a href="#source-1">1</a></sup> The premium has to be visible from the first week, or it becomes a discount problem in reverse.

## Stop buying members with discounts

Discounts fill the calendar but bring in weaker members. Research outside fitness is consistent: customers won with a 35% discount were worth about **half as much** over time as full-price customers,<sup class="fn"><a href="#source-13">13</a></sup> and customers won with free trials had **59% lower lifetime value**.<sup class="fn"><a href="#source-14">14</a></sup> These studies cover newspapers, groceries and digital TV, not gyms, so treat them as a strong analogy. Gyms already lose many members early: in one study of a single gym, 63% of new members stopped before the third month.<sup class="fn"><a href="#source-15">15</a></sup> Discounted joiners make that worse, not better.

A fractional CFO writing for Athletech News put the studio version simply: when intro offers never end, a $16 list price turns into a $12 average seat, and that is a discounting problem, not a marketing one.<sup class="fn"><a href="#source-17">17</a></sup>

## Your rollout checklist

1. **Write down what the premium tier adds** in one sentence a member would repeat. If you can't, it isn't different enough yet.
2. **Keep current members on their price** and invite them to upgrade.
3. **Schedule the extras** (check-ins, small-group slots) in the booking system from day one.
4. **End open-ended intro offers.** Keep one time-limited trial at most.
5. **Track monthly:** tier mix, revenue per member, churn by tier, and the share of premium perks actually used. Review after 90 days.
