Germany is now the largest gym market in Europe, and it got there by growing at both ends at once. **12.36 million people held a membership at the end of 2025**, up 5.6% in a year and the first time the market has cleared 12 million (DSSV, Eckdaten der deutschen Fitnesswirtschaft 2026). Count the **1.47 million people who train through aggregators** such as EGYM Wellpass, Hansefit, Wellhub and Urban Sports Club, and **13.83 million people train in German facilities**.

Revenue grew faster than members. **Net revenue reached 6.25 billion euros, up 7.4%**, which means operators earned more per member, not just from more of them. The reason is in the price list.

## The price list: 48.55 euros, and the gap is widening

The **average 12-month standard membership costs 48.55 euros a month (gross)**, up from 46.95 euros in 2024, a rise of 3.4%. That average hides three very different markets:

- **Chains: 40.54 euros** (+2.10 euros in a year, the fastest rise)
- **Independent clubs: 59.24 euros** (+1.39 euros)
- **Special-interest studios (EMS, Pilates, boutique): 80.02 euros** (down from 82.79 euros, because fewer high-priced EMS studios remain; EMS alone still averages 107.41 euros)

Read that again from the independent operator's chair. **Your members already pay 46% more than a chain member**, and they keep paying: 52.1% of independent-club members sit in the 45 to 65 euro band. Chains are the ones raising prices fastest, and 23.7% of their members now also sit in that band, up from 17.5%. The whole market is moving up. Discounting into it is the wrong direction.

## Who owns the market: chains hold half the revenue

Deloitte's **Der deutsche Fitnessmarkt 2026** splits the 9,647 facilities three ways:

| Segment | Facilities | Members | Net revenue | Share of revenue |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Independents | 4,202 (43.6%) | 4.30M | 2.65bn € | 42.4% |
| Chains | 3,002 (31.1%) | 7.59M | 3.17bn € | 50.7% |
| Special interest | 2,443 (25.3%) | 0.47M | 0.43bn € | 6.9% |

**Chains run less than a third of the sites and book more than half the money.** Chain revenue grew 9.3% in 2025, ahead of the market. The **top 10 chains alone hold 5.77 million members, 46.7% of the entire market**, the highest share Deloitte has ever recorded in 23 editions.

The table of ten changed in one deal. **Basic-Fit bought clever fit, adding about 797,000 members and 493 sites**, the largest fitness transaction in the study's history. Basic-Fit now has 444 German sites, more than RSG Group (213) and FitX (112) combined, and ranks third by members (829,000) behind **RSG Group (1.37 million) and FitX (about 1.0 million)**. LifeFit Group (+64.8%) and all inclusive Fitness (+44.5%) grew fastest among the big ten. Europe saw 27 major deals in 2025 and German players were involved in nine of them.

If you run one or two sites, this is the context for every decision you make in 2026: **60.9% of independents already describe competitive pressure as strong or rather strong**, up from 55.8%.

## Penetration: large market, still room

**14.8% of the German population holds a gym membership**, up a full percentage point in a year. Count aggregator users and it is 16.5%. Among people aged 15 to 65 it is 21.9%, which means **78% of working-age Germans are still not members**.

Europe as a whole sits at 9.3% penetration and the **US at 24.9%** (HFA, via the EuropeActive / Deloitte report). Germany leads Europe in absolute members, **12.4 million against 12.2 million in the UK**, while the UK grew faster (+6.6%). Europe counted 75.5 million members and 39.1 billion euros in revenue in 2025. The DACH region together has **15.16 million members across 12,495 facilities** (Germany 12.36M, Switzerland 1.45M, Austria 1.35M).

## Who the members are

Statista's edition of the Allensbach AWA survey (2023 data, study 6326) gives the profile. **Women are 45% of gym-goers: 8.99 million women against 10.95 million men.** 31.7% of men and 25.3% of women go to a gym at least occasionally. Age is the surprise for anyone still marketing to twenty-somethings:

| Age | Gym-goers |
|---|---|
| 14 to 19 | 1.58M |
| 20 to 29 | 4.65M |
| 30 to 39 | 4.20M |
| 40 to 49 | 3.14M |
| 50 to 59 | 3.08M |
| 60 to 69 | 2.08M |
| 70 and older | 1.21M |

**9.5 million of 19.9 million gym-goers are 40 or older, almost half.** The DSSV adds that demand is growing fastest among under-30s and over-60s. Statista has published the 2025 AWA edition of both tables (updated 10 Oct 2025) behind its paywall; we will swap these figures the moment we have it.

Motives match the age curve. In Statista Consumer Insights' survey of about 400 German gym-goers, **63% join to be and stay fit, 55% to build muscle, 46% for a healthy, active lifestyle and 40% to reduce stress**. Looking good (40%) and losing weight (36%) come last. Among facilities, **39.3% now position themselves as health clubs**, ahead of training (33.5%) and lifestyle (21.9%).

## The money going back in

Operators are spending. **The sector invested 798 million euros net in 2025**, about 83,000 euros per facility, and **91.9% of facilities invested**, up from 82.2%. Strength equipment led (57.5% of facilities), then cardio (45.2%), small equipment (40.2%) and diagnostics (30.1%). For 2026, 80% plan to invest again and the planned volume is **1.06 billion euros, the first time above a billion**.

The sector employs **167,100 people (+6.0%)** and 95.3% of operators pay for staff training. 72.3% of chains and 58.9% of independents offer digital extras. Online contract sales reached 24% of new contracts in 2024, up from 14% the year before.

## What operators and coaches should take from this

**Price for the market you are in, not the one you fear.** The average member pays more every year and independent members pay most. The 18.70 euro gap between you and a chain is your coaching, your diagnostics and your community. Name it on the price list.

**Use aggregators to fill the floor, then convert.** Three in four facilities are already connected. Deloitte estimates 20 to 30% of aggregator members are inactive and the whole channel is worth 700 to 800 million euros against 6.25 billion in direct revenue. It is a funnel, not a business model.

**Build for the 40-plus member.** Nearly half of gym-goers are over 40, the fastest growth is among over-60s, and health is the leading motive. Strength, mobility, back pain and blood pressure are the product. The chains are weakest here.

**Pick your side of the consolidation.** With the top 10 holding 46.7% of members and Basic-Fit now the largest operator by sites, the independent that survives is the one a chain cannot copy: a specialism, a coach-led culture and members who pay 59 euros because they get something for it.
