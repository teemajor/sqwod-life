The story everyone tells about fitness equipment goes like this: a pandemic boom, then a crash. The wholesale data says otherwise. **There was a boom. There was no crash.** And the real winner was not the connected bike. It was the humble dumbbell.

## Strength took the market

US wholesale sales of **home free weights rose from $201M in 2019 to $561M in 2023, up 179%** (SFIA via Statista). Exercise bikes more than doubled, from $472M to $1,028M. Home gyms went from $221M to $463M.

Meanwhile, **elliptical sales fell from $904M to $807M, down 11%**. The machine that defined the 2000s home gym is shrinking.

The pattern is clear: people bought tools for strength and simple, intense cardio. They did not buy more of the old, single-purpose cardio machines.

## Home reset higher, then started to cool

Total **US home fitness equipment sales hit a record $6.56bn in 2023**, up 65% on 2019 ($3.98bn). Sales jumped to $5.59bn in 2020 and $6.42bn in 2021, dipped to $6.08bn in 2022, then set a new high. That is not a crash. It is a new, higher floor.

But the newest data shows the turn. SFIA's 2026 report, covering 2025, says **parts of the consumer/home segment declined, while institutional equipment saw broad-based growth.** Overall US sporting goods wholesale sales reached **$130bn in 2025, up 3.7% on the year and 34.7% since 2020.**

## Gyms are buying again

Gym and club equipment had the harder pandemic. **Institutional wholesale sales fell 17% in 2020, from $1,566M to $1,302M.** By 2023 they had climbed to **a record $1,693M**, 8% above the pre-pandemic peak.

In 2025, SFIA describes that growth as broad-based, driven by continued investment in gyms, health clubs and commercial facilities. Read together: the home boom built the habit, and facilities are now spending to win those people back.

The long-range picture is steady rather than explosive. **Statista Market Insights forecasts global exercise equipment revenue to rise from $46.7bn in 2024 to $59.5bn in 2028**, roughly 6% a year. In the US, exercise equipment revenue is forecast to grow from $5.7bn to $6.8bn over the same period.

## What operators and coaches should take from this

**Your client probably owns weights.** Plan for it. Hybrid programs that use their home kit keep the relationship alive between sessions and make your coaching part of their week, not just your floor.

**Build floor space around strength.** The demand data points one way: racks, benches, dumbbells and plates over rows of ellipticals.

**Equipment is no longer the reason to show up.** When home strength gear sells at record levels, access alone does not sell. Coaching, privacy, atmosphere and community do. That is why a private, fully equipped training room with a coach is a stronger offer than a membership to a room full of machines.

**Watch your costs.** SFIA highlights trade and tariff pressure across the industry. If new equipment is on your plan for 2027, get quotes early and compare buying with leasing.
