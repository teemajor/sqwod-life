## The Graveyard Shift Nobody Talks About

Row House is gone. The doors are locked, the rowers are somewhere in a truck, and the landlord at Winterfield Crossing is back on the phone. Sound familiar? It should. Spaces shutter, leases flip, and the next concept moves in before the chalk dust settles.

But here's the thing most coaches miss when they see that "Space Available" sign go up: the *community* that trained there doesn't disappear. It just becomes homeless. And whoever shows up first with a clear identity and a warm welcome wins those members.

That's exactly what's playing out in Richmond, where a Pilates studio called Jetset is moving into the former Row House location at Winterfield Crossing, according to Richmond BizSense. Same square footage, totally different method. Smart move — or lucky timing? Neither. It's a positioning decision.

## Recovery, Wellness, and the Membership Nobody Is Selling

Meanwhile, up in Thurston County, local officials are spending National Wellness Month and National Recovery Month spotlighting community organizations doing the quiet, unglamorous work of keeping people healthy, according to the Nisqually Valley News.

Notice what those two things have in common: *community organizations.* Not gyms. Not studios. Organizations.

That word choice matters. The facilities that survive lease turnovers and trend cycles aren't the ones with the best equipment. They're the ones that made themselves indispensable to a specific group of people — people in recovery, parents back from mat leave, seniors who need low-impact movement, athletes rebuilding after injury. When your studio is a *community organization* in your members' minds, nobody leaves just because a shinier box opens down the street.

Jetset didn't just find an empty room. They stepped into a gap in the wellness ecosystem of that neighborhood. That's the move.

## What You Should Do Before Monday Morning

You don't need a new lease to think like this. You need two lists and one conversation.

**List one:** Write down every underserved group within a ten-minute drive of your facility. People in recovery. Older adults. New moms. Shift workers. People who've been told by a doctor to "move more" but have never once felt welcome in a gym.

**List two:** Write down every local organization — recovery centers, community health clinics, faith communities, workplace wellness programs — that already serves those groups but has no fitness partner.

**The conversation:** Pick one organization from list two and reach out this week. Not with a pitch deck. With a question: *"What does your community actually need that they're not getting?"*

That question is how a studio stops being a room with equipment and starts being the kind of place Thurston County officials put in a press release during Wellness Month.

When the next Row House closes in your market — and one will — the coaches who've built those relationships won't be scrambling for members. They'll be the ones the displaced community calls first.

The space is never the asset. The people are.
