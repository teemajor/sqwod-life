## The Industry Just Had a Collective Epiphany

Something clicked this week across gyms, investors, and wellness researchers — and if you miss it, you'll spend the next two years wondering why your churn rate won't budge.

The Global Wellness Institute put it plainly: belonging drives mental wellbeing at work. Not perks. Not ping-pong tables. *Belonging.* Now look at everything else that dropped this week through that lens. It reframes all of it.

## Everyone's Betting on Community With a Hook

At the HCM Summit, Everyone Active's Duncan Jefford revealed a referral partnership with Chemists 4U, reported by Health Club Management. A pharmacy sending people to a gym. Think about what that signals: fitness operators are no longer waiting for motivated people to walk through the door. They're meeting people inside their existing trusted relationships — health, care, community — and pulling them in.

Meanwhile in Stuttgart, JOHN REED just opened what fitness MANAGEMENT is calling a Fitness Music Club. Not a gym. A *club*. The naming is deliberate. Music, identity, tribe. That's the product.

And then there's Mark Wahlberg's Municipal Gym, eyeing an affordable longevity positioning ahead of launch, per Athletech News. Longevity is hot, but *affordable* longevity is the move. It signals a play for regular people who want to live longer, not just wealthy biohackers. That's a community-of-many, not a community-of-few.

On the capital side, HCM Invest brought investors and fitness businesses together in London — again reported by Health Club Management — which tells you money is actively circling this space, looking for the operators who've cracked community at scale.

Even Wilde landing $40M to scale protein snacks, flagged by Fitt Insider, fits. Snacks don't raise that kind of capital on taste alone. They raise it on identity — the kind of product people buy because of *who they are*, not just what they need after a workout.

## Your One Move This Week

Stop marketing your gym as a place to get fit. Start marketing it as a place to *belong to something*.

Here's what that looks like in practice:

**Audit your referral ecosystem.** Everyone Active didn't wait for organic word-of-mouth — they built a structured pipeline through a pharmacy partner. Who in your local area already has the trust of your ideal member? A GP surgery. A mental health charity. A corporate HR team. Map three potential referral partners and reach out to one this week.

**Rename one thing.** JOHN REED didn't open a gym — they opened a club. Language shapes perception. Look at your class names, your membership tiers, your welcome email. Does any of it sound like belonging? If it sounds like a price list, rewrite it.

**Pick a purpose lane.** Municipal is going affordable longevity. JOHN REED is going music and identity. Vague gyms lose. Specific gyms win. What does your member tell their friend your gym is *for*?

The capital is following this thesis. The operators who frame fitness as community infrastructure — not just equipment and classes — are the ones getting meetings in London and partnerships with pharmacies. The belonging economy is real, and it's funding the next wave of fitness businesses.

Get specific about who your gym is for, or someone else will.
