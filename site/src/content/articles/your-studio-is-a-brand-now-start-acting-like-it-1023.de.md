## The Market Just Told You Something

Christie's auction houses just pulled in $289 million across London sales, signaling — according to صوت الإمارات — a recovery in the global art market. That's not a fitness story. Until you squint.

What moves art prices? Scarcity, identity, and the feeling that owning a piece says something about you. Sound familiar? Because the fitness brands winning right now are playing the exact same game.

Club Pilates is targeting 1,500 locations, per Club Solutions. RockSalt Pilates just hired a COO and is gearing up for franchise expansion, per Athletech News. Fitness On Demand added 99 Mvmnt workouts to deepen its digital library, per the Health & Fitness Association. Three very different companies, one shared instinct: the era of the generic fitness class is over. Members don't buy reps. They buy belonging.

## What's Really Happening Here

Club Pilates didn't build toward 1,500 locations by being the cheapest mat in the room. Tianna Strateman's story, as told to Club Solutions, is about consistent brand experience at scale — the same vibe, the same promise, every single location.

RockSalt Pilates goes further. Music-driven classes aren't a gimmick. They're a positioning decision. Walk into a RockSalt session and you know immediately it's not for everyone — which is exactly why it's for someone. That someone comes back.

Fitness On Demand's move is the digital parallel. Ninety-nine new Mvmnt workouts isn't just more content. It's a signal that operators need programming depth to serve members who've decided what kind of mover they are. Variety isn't the sell anymore. Fit is.

The Christie's parallel holds: collectors don't pay premiums for paintings that could hang anywhere. They pay for the ones that feel like theirs. Your members are making the same calculation every time they choose a class.

## Do This Before Your Next Class

You don't need a franchise deal or a COO to steal these lessons. You need a cleaner answer to one question your members are already asking subconsciously: *What does training here say about me?*

Here's where to start:

**Name your lane.** RockSalt chose music. Club Pilates chose method consistency. Pick one thing that makes your programming unmistakably yours and say it out loud — in your class intro, your social bio, your intake form.

**Audit your programming depth.** Fitness On Demand's Mvmnt expansion tells you members want options within a style, not a different style every week. If you coach strength, do you have a clear beginner track, a competition track, a maintenance track? Depth keeps people. Novelty brings them in once.

**Make the experience repeatable.** The franchise model works because you know what you're getting. Your best members are already your franchise. Give them the same quality signal every session — same warm-up structure, same coaching cues, same vibe — so they can confidently refer friends.

The fitness market is bifurcating fast: big branded experiences on one end, deeply personal coaching on the other. The middle — the generic gym class with no story — is where attention goes to die.

Pick a side. Build the brand around it. The collectors will find you.
