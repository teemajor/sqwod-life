## OneSpaWorld Reports Its 22nd Straight Record Quarter

OneSpaWorld, the operator of spas, fitness and wellness centers aboard cruise ships and at destination resorts, posted record third-quarter revenue of $276.5 million, up 7% year over year, according to [Athletech News](https://athletechnews.com/onespaworld-posts-record-q3-revenue-as-cruise-wellness-business-surges/). Net income rose 16% to $28.2 million and adjusted EBITDA climbed 12% to $39.2 million, both quarterly records. The company has now delivered 22 consecutive quarters of year-over-year revenue and adjusted EBITDA growth. On the back of those results, OneSpaWorld raised its full-year outlook, now targeting revenue of between $1.033 billion and $1.038 billion and adjusted EBITDA of $138 million to $140 million.

## What a Billion-Dollar Wellness Cruise Operator Tells Land-Based Gyms

OneSpaWorld operates in a segment most studio owners rarely think of as competition: captive-audience wellness at sea. But the numbers matter on land too. The company's sustained growth reflects what its CEO Leonard Fluxman called "the durability of our business model" built around delivering memorable health and wellness experiences. That framing should resonate with any studio operator. Consumers are willing to spend on wellness when the experience feels worth it, and they are doing so across a growing range of formats and settings. For context, the Sqwod Deal Tracker has recorded more than 30 gym and studio deals since January 2026, with $447 million disclosed across transactions involving operators such as FitLab, PureGym, and others. OneSpaWorld's billion-dollar annual revenue run rate sits well above what most of those deals represent individually, illustrating the scale gap between traditional studio operators and vertically integrated wellness businesses that control their environment end to end.

## Sqwod's Take: Experience-Led Wellness Is Winning, and It Is Not Waiting for Studios to Catch Up

OneSpaWorld is not a gym chain, but it is competing for the same discretionary wellness spend that studio and gym operators depend on. Its model works because guests are immersed in the experience with no friction, no commute, and no competing priorities. Land-based operators cannot replicate a cruise ship, but the lesson is clear: studios that treat a visit as a transaction will keep losing ground to formats that treat it as an experience. The winners here are integrated wellness operators with pricing power and differentiated environments. The losers are undifferentiated studios still competing on class counts and monthly fees. OneSpaWorld's 22-quarter streak is not an outlier; it is a signal about where consumer priorities have been heading for years.
