The Google Fitbit Edge, announced on 12 October 2026 and on sale from 27 October, replaces the Charge 6: according to Bloomberg, Google is retiring the Charge line. On paper it is a clear step up for training: a 1.34-inch AMOLED that is about 46% larger, built-in GPS that Google says is 40% more accurate, an altimeter, more than 50 workout modes with structured workouts, and automatic detection of up to 100 activity types. Battery is rated at up to seven days and, unusually for a tracker, the battery is user-replaceable.

The trade-offs: NFC and Google Wallet are gone, Charge 6 bands don't fit, and the price rises to 179.99 EUR. The Gemini-based Google Health Coach sits behind Google Health Premium (8.99 EUR a month after three free months). Our scores come from specs and early hands-on coverage only. No owner ratings or scored reviews exist yet, so treat this page as provisional.
